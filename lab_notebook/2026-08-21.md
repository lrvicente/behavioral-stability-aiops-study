## Root cause: OOM termination of the cron scheduler

Fault injection stopped on two hosts in mid-July and did not resume. Kernel and
systemd evidence collected 2026-08-20:

| host | last START | class | cron.service failed | last END |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cnsm-web-01 | 2026-07-11 21:43:01 | memory_pressure | 2026-07-11 21:43:58 | 21:57:24 |
| cnsm-web-04 | 2026-07-14 10:50:01 | memory_pressure | 2026-07-14 10:50:26 | 11:34:04 |

Both events used `vm_bytes=8G` on hosts with 7 GB of RAM. The kernel log shows
`oom-kill: constraint=CONSTRAINT_NONE, global_oom,
task_memcg=/system.slice/cron.service, task=stress-ng-vm` with
`oom_score_adj:1000` and `anon-rss` around 4.2 GB per worker. `systemctl status
cron` reported `Active: failed (Result: oom-kill)` on both hosts, dated to
those timestamps.

Sequence: the injector runs as a descendant of `cron.service`, so its stress
processes are accounted to that unit's control group. The injector had marked
those processes with `oom_score_adj=1000`, which is correct and worked: the
kernel selected stress-ng as the victim rather than nginx or the Zabbix agent.
Unit-level OOM policy then stopped the whole `cron.service` unit. The injector
process already launched survived and wrote a normal END at the scheduled
completion time.

Consequence for the evidence chain: both execution logs record their final
event as complete and well-formed. The failure is invisible within the log
itself. It is detectable only by reconciling the deterministic schedule against
the execution log, which is what surfaced it on 2026-08-20.

Both hosts had rebooted shortly before, cnsm-web-01 at 2026-07-09 01:29:19 and
cnsm-web-04 at 2026-07-08 22:17:54, during the monitoring master outage window.
Cron ran normally for two to five days after those reboots, so the reboots are
not the proximate cause. Whether the post-reboot memory state made these two
hosts more OOM-prone than the other ten is not established.

Both hosts continued reporting telemetry throughout, so the monitoring plane
never surfaced the failure.

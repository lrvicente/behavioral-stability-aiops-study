## Master Zabbix service outage (2026-07-08 to 2026-07-18): documented retroactively

Between 2026-07-08 21:56 UTC and 2026-07-18 15:16 UTC the master Zabbix server on cnsm2027-master was not running, resulting in a 10-day gap in the study history table and in the nightly OVH object storage exports (which produced empty 39-byte gzip files for 2026-07-09 through 2026-07-17). The service was found in systemd state disabled during the health check on 2026-07-18. A kernel-upgrade reboot on 2026-07-08 at 23:59 UTC (kernel 6.8.0-86 to 6.8.0-134) did not restart the service because it was already in the disabled state. The transition from enabled to disabled occurred between 2026-07-03 and 2026-07-08 and its exact cause was not identified. The service was re-enabled and started on 2026-07-18 at 15:16 UTC. Collection has been continuous since then.

During the gap the fault injection cron continued to execute autonomously on all 12 study hosts and on cnsm-gpu-01. Execution logs at /var/log/cnsm-faults/executions.log on each host preserve the record of which faults were injected and when, so the pre-registered schedule fidelity is documentable independently of the master telemetry.

## Decision on the July gap: accepted as threat to validity, no recovery

Two options were considered: recover the 10-day gap from the parallel homelab Zabbix instance (which monitors the same 12 study hosts and has the three custom ground-truth items cnsm.workload.heartbeat, cnsm.fault.active, cnsm.fault.class), or accept the gap and document it as an operational threat to validity in downstream papers.

Decision on 2026-08-01: accept the gap. The homelab instance runs Zabbix 8.0.0alpha2, whereas the master runs 7.0 LTS. Recovering data from a different (alpha) version and mixing it into a canonical dataset that was pre-registered as coming from the master would require item-by-item equivalence validation, schema conversion, and an additional pre-registration amendment. The scientific value of the 10 recovered days (11% of a single cycle, later diluted by cycles 2 and 3 for a total of 270 collection days in the three-cycle plan) does not justify the methodological complexity and the reviewer questions that would arise from a mixed-source dataset. Analyses of cycle 1 will use the 80-day continuous dataset (2026-05-15 to 2026-07-08 and 2026-07-18 to 2026-08-13) and this decision will be reported in the threats-to-validity section of any downstream paper.

The execution logs on the hosts remain the reference for what the pre-registered schedule dispatched during the gap. Schedule adherence can still be evidenced from these logs even without master telemetry.
